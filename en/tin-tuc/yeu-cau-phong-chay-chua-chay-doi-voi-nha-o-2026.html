Fire Safety Requirements for Homes in Vietnam 2026

Quan Construction · 29/10/2026

From 1 July 2025, Law No. 55/2024/QH15 on Fire Prevention, Fighting and Rescue (the Fire Law) and Decree 105/2025/ND-CP replaced the old rules. The Law has a separate article on fire prevention for homes (Article 20), applying to ordinary family homes. This article summarizes what homeowners need to do, with links to the original text.

1. What is the household head responsible for?

Under Clause 6, Article 8 of the Fire Law, the head of a household directly using a home must:

  • Implement the safety conditions in Article 20 (homes) and Article 21 (homes combined with production or business);
  • Promote, urge and remind other family members to comply with fire law;
  • Regularly self-inspect and promptly remedy risks of fire, explosion, accidents and incidents.

If the home is rented or lent: the landlord must remind the tenant; and the tenant, borrower or occupant bears the same responsibility as the household head, unless the parties agree otherwise (Clause 8, Article 8).

2. Fire prevention conditions: electricity, kitchen, worship areas

Under Clause 1, Article 20, a home must ensure:

  • Safe installation and use of electrical equipment: wiring and electrical equipment in the home must be electrically safe; regularly inspect and promptly repair or replace unsafe wires and equipment (Point c, Clause 1, Article 24).
  • Safe arrangement of the cooking area, worship area and burning of votive paper; no flammable or explosive items or substances near fire or heat sources.
  • Electric vehicle charging: only charging equipment meeting national technical regulations; the charging place in the home must have a solution ensuring fire safety (Point d, Clause 1, Article 24).

3. Fire fighting and escape conditions

Under Clause 2, Article 20, a home must:

  • Have fire prevention and fighting equipment appropriate to its actual capacity and conditions, ready for fire fighting and escape;
  • Arrange and maintain escape routes, emergency exits or passages that ensure escape.

The Law strictly prohibits obstructing escape routes or disabling escape routes, escape paths or fire spread barriers (Clause 10, Article 14). When building or renovating, plan a second exit in advance (balcony, window, roof access) and avoid completely sealing openings with fixed metal frames or cages.

4. Homes in centrally run cities: fire alarm transmission devices

Under Clause 5, Article 20, homes in centrally run cities located in areas without adequate traffic infrastructure or fire-fighting water sources must be equipped with a fire extinguisher and a fire alarm transmission device connected to the fire prevention database system. Homes in other areas are encouraged to be so equipped.

  • Timeline: no later than 1 July 2027 (Clause 4, Article 54).
  • The city People's Committee must publish the list of areas and homes covered by this requirement by 1 January 2026 at the latest and ensure funding for equipping and connecting fire alarm transmission devices (Clause 1, Article 27 of Decree 105/2025/ND-CP).
  • The household head is responsible for keeping the device operating and paying the maintenance costs (Point c, Clause 1, Article 25).

5. Self-inspecting fire safety at home: what to check and how often

Under Point h, Clause 2, Article 13 of Decree 105/2025/ND-CP, the household head (or tenant, borrower or occupant) regularly self-inspects: the provision and working order of fire prevention and fighting equipment; safety in using fire sources, heat sources and flammable substances; and maintenance of escape routes and emergency exits. Homes covered in section 4 must also inspect the fire alarm transmission device.

The household head decides the timing, scope and way of recording results, but no less often than once a month (Clause 1, Article 14). In addition, competent authorities may conduct surprise inspections of homes when there are signs of violations, complaints or denunciations (Clause 2, Article 13).

6. When building new, renovating or doing business at home

  • During construction, the site must have fire safety rules, signs, suitable fire-fighting equipment and a designated person in charge of fire safety; the investor and the contractor share responsibility (Article 19).
  • Where fire safety regulations and standards are referenced for the home, follow those regulations and standards (Clause 3, Article 20). Houses of 7 floors or more or with 2 or more basements must be designed in compliance with fire safety regulations - see Designing your own house 2026: when to hire a designer.
  • Homes combined with production or business must meet additional conditions in Article 21; converting or adding functions to a building without ensuring fire safety is strictly prohibited (Clause 8, Article 14).
This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. The 2024 Fire Law and Decree 105/2025/ND-CP have had some articles amended; when applying them, check the current documents and the guidance of the commune or ward police where the home is located.
