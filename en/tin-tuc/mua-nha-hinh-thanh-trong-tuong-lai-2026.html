Buying Off-Plan Housing in Vietnam 2026: Conditions, Deposits

Quan Construction · 10/11/2026

Buying an apartment or townhouse when a project has just started construction (buying "off-plan") is riskier than buying a ready-built home. This article summarizes the buyer-protection rules under Real Estate Business Law No. 29/2023/QH15 (effective from 1 August 2024) and Decree 96/2024/ND-CP.

1. What conditions must the project meet to be sold?

Under Article 24 of the Real Estate Business Law, housing to be formed in the future may be put into business only when it meets the following main conditions:

  • The housing has started construction under construction law.
  • There are land use right documents: a land allocation decision, a land lease decision with a land lease contract, a decision permitting a change of land use purpose, or a certificate.
  • There is a building permit with the permit application file (if a permit is required), or a commencement notice with the design file (if exempt from a permit).
  • There are documents on acceptance of technical infrastructure corresponding to the project progress; for apartment buildings and mixed-use buildings with housing, the foundation must have passed acceptance.
  • It is in a project approved for sale or lease-purchase; not in dispute and not seized; and information has been publicly disclosed under Article 6.

Before selling, the investor must send a notice to the provincial real estate business management authority. Within 15 days, that authority checks and replies in writing whether the housing meets the conditions for sale and posts it on the housing and real estate market information portal (Clause 4, Article 24; Article 8 of Decree 96/2024/ND-CP).

2. Deposit: maximum 5%, and only when the project is eligible

The investor may collect a deposit of no more than 5% of the sale price, and only when the housing already meets the conditions to be put into business. The deposit agreement must state the sale price (Clause 5, Article 23). The investor also may not authorize other organizations or individuals (including trading floors and brokers) to sign deposit contracts or sale contracts for housing to be formed in the future (Point d, Clause 4, Article 23). Collecting money contrary to the rules is a prohibited act (Clause 5, Article 8).

3. How much can be paid before receiving the house?

Under Article 25 of the Real Estate Business Law, for a sale contract:

  • The first payment is no more than 30% of the contract value, including the deposit.
  • Later payments must match the construction progress; in total no more than 70% before the house is handed over (if the seller is an economic organization with foreign investment capital, no more than 50%).
  • While the buyer has not yet been issued a certificate (sổ hồng), the seller may not collect more than 95%; the remaining 5% is paid when the buyer is issued the certificate.

For a lease-purchase contract: the first payment is no more than 30%, and total advance payments no more than 50% of the contract value.

4. Bank guarantee: protection if the investor delays handover

Under Article 26, before selling, the investor must have a bank's approval to guarantee the obligation to refund advance payments received (and other sums under the contract, if any) if it fails to hand over the house as committed. Buyers should note:

  • When signing the contract, the investor must send the buyer a copy of the bank's commitment letter to issue the guarantee.
  • The buyer may choose to have or not have a guarantee; if the buyer declines, this must be agreed in writing at the time of signing the contract.
  • The investor delivers the guarantee letter within 10 working days of the contract signing date (or another period agreed by the parties), and may receive payment only after the buyer has received the guarantee letter.
  • If the investor does not hand over the house or does not refund the full amount, the buyer may request the bank to pay under the guarantee letter.
  • This guarantee rule does not apply to buying or lease-purchasing social housing.

5. Contract, notarization and handover

A contract for sale of housing to be formed in the future may be signed only when the housing meets the conditions to be put into business. A contract in which at least one party is a real estate business enterprise is notarized or authenticated at the request of the parties, not mandatory (Clauses 3 and 4, Article 44). The investor must use the buyer's money for the proper purpose and provide construction progress information under the contract (Clause 4, Article 23). Handover of the housing follows housing law (Article 27).

6. Checklist before paying

  1. The provincial authority's document confirming the housing meets the conditions for sale.
  2. Land documents, building permit or commencement notice, and foundation acceptance (apartments).
  3. Whether the project or unit is mortgaged (Point e, Clause 3, Article 6).
  4. The bank's commitment letter to issue a guarantee; receive the guarantee letter before paying the first installment.
  5. The contract is signed directly with the investor; deposit up to 5%, first installment up to 30% (including the deposit).

When receiving the house and processing the certificate, see also Land and house registration fee 2026.

This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. The 2023 Real Estate Business Law and Decree 96/2024/ND-CP are currently partly no longer in force; the cited articles do not appear to have been amended in our legal library, but when transacting, check the current documents and ask the Department of Construction where the project is located.
