From 1 July 2026, construction investment projects are managed under Construction Law No. 135/2025/QH15 and its guiding decrees: Decree 217/2026/ND-CP (management of construction activities), Decree 207/2026/ND-CP (quality, construction, maintenance), Decree 206/2026/ND-CP (costs) and Decree 210/2026/ND-CP (contracts). This handbook arranges the tasks in the order prescribed by law so that project practitioners can look them up quickly.

1. The three stages and project classification

Under Clause 1, Article 16 of the Construction Law 2025, the construction investment sequence has 3 stages: project preparation, project implementation and construction closeout. A project may be phased, or split into component projects or independent compensation, support and resettlement projects (Clause 2).

Before going through each step, you need to determine which type of project it is, because procedures differ by funding source (Article 17):

Project type (by investment form)Points to note
Public investment projectMost strictly managed: appraisal at the specialized construction agency, cost management under Decree 206/2026, contracts under Decree 210/2026, capital final settlement under Decree 193/2026.
PPP projectAppraised and adjusted under the law on investment in the form of public-private partnership; many construction matters are still governed by the Construction Law.
Projects using recurrent expenditure or other State budget funds outside public investmentFollow the provisions on public investment projects in the Construction Law (Point c, Clause 1, Article 17).
Business investment project (other funds)The investment decision-maker organizes the appraisal itself; appraisal at the specialized agency is required only for large-scale projects or those with works that significantly affect safety or community interests.

Classification by scale (nationally important, Group A, B, C) follows the criteria of the law on public investment; classification by purpose and function follows Appendix III of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP.

When is only an Economic-Technical Report needed? Under Clause 2, Article 27 of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP, these are: projects for religious purposes; projects with total investment not exceeding VND 40 billion (excluding compensation, site clearance and land use fees); repair projects for maintenance purposes of Group C scale; dredging for maintenance of public maritime channels and inland waterways; projects that mainly involve equipment procurement and installation with construction costs below 10% of total investment and not exceeding VND 20 billion (except nationally important, Group A and PPP projects). A project that requires only an Economic-Technical Report does not need appraisal at the specialized construction agency (Clause 2, Article 27 of the Construction Law).

Which projects must be appraised at the specialized construction agency? Public investment projects, PPP projects, large-scale business investment projects (those whose investment policy is approved by the National Assembly or the Prime Minister, or that meet the nationally important criteria) and business investment projects with works listed in Appendix IV (Clause 1, Article 32 of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP).

Not every project is carried out strictly step by step. For projects not covered by Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of Article 4, the investment decision-maker may decide to carry out the tasks of the implementation and closeout stages sequentially or concurrently, in line with the project approval decision (Clause 5, Article 4 of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP).

Commencement conditions – checklist

  1. A construction site is available for handover, in whole or in part.
  2. A building permit has been issued (unless exempt under Article 43).
  3. Approved construction drawings are available for the part or item of the works to be commenced.
  4. A contract has been signed with the contractors carrying out the relevant construction activities according to the schedule.
  5. A commencement notice has been sent, in writing or online, to the local state management agency for construction (except for individual houses of households and individuals).

Basis: Clause 1, Article 48 of the Construction Law 2025; the commencement notice form is in Appendix V and the update to the national database is under Article 12 of Decree 207/2026/ND-CP. Emergency and urgent works, special public investment projects and works that must start early on the Prime Minister's direction need only have a construction site (Clause 2, Article 48).

4. Construction closeout stage

Under Point c, Clause 1, Article 4 of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP, this includes: final settlement of the construction contract (if any), final settlement of construction investment capital, works warranty and handover of related documents.

TaskKey timelineLegal basis

The time for preparing the capital final settlement file is counted from the date the project or works is handed over for operation and use (or the effective date of the document permitting suspension of implementation) to the date the complete file is submitted to the agency leading the verification (Clause 1, Article 21 of Decree 193/2026/ND-CP). Decree 193/2026/ND-CP applies only to projects using public investment capital, recurrent State budget expenditure and other State budget funds (Article 1); projects with other funding are settled under agreements and enterprise regulations.

5. Project management: which form to choose?

Project management organization forms are prescribed in Article 32 of the Construction Law 2025 and detailed in Articles 44–48 of Decree 217/2026/ND-CP:

  • Specialized or regional construction investment project management board (Article 45).
  • Hiring a project management consultant (Article 46).
  • Project management board under the investor (Article 47).
  • Investor self-performs project management (Article 48).

Project management content covers scope, work plan, quantities, quality, schedule, cost, safety, environment, contractor selection, contracts, risk and information (Article 33; schedule: Article 34).

7. Common sequencing errors

  • Commencing when the construction drawings have not been approved or the commencement notice has not been sent.
  • Building a part of the works not covered by the permit, or adjusting the design in a way that changes the permit content without adjusting the permit – see adjusting and extending building permits.
  • Not verifying the design for works that must be verified under Clause 5, Article 26 of the Construction Law.
  • Putting works into use before completion acceptance, or without a document approving the acceptance results for works subject to inspection (Art. 29, Decree 207).
  • Late preparation of the capital final settlement file beyond the period in Article 21 of Decree 193/2026.
This is a summary for reference and does not replace legal documents. Documents on public investment, bidding and PPP are not yet in the Quan Construction library, so they are only named. Transitional projects follow the transitional provisions of each document.
