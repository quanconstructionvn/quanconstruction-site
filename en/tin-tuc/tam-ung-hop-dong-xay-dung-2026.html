Advance Payments in Construction Contracts 2026: Limits, Guarantees

Quan Construction · 12/11/2026

When signing a contract to build a house, the contractor often asks the homeowner to pay a sum in advance to buy materials and mobilize labor. From 1 July 2026, this payment is governed by Article 12 of Decree 210/2026/ND-CP on construction contracts (replacing Decree 37/2015/ND-CP).

1. What is a construction contract advance?

An advance is a sum that the employer (homeowner, investor) pays in advance, interest-free, to the contractor to start work under the contract (Clause 1, Article 12). An advance is not payment for work already done, but is deducted gradually through later payments.

2. Private houses: the parties agree themselves

For ordinary contracts (such as a homeowner hiring a contractor to build a house), Clause 2, Article 12 allows the parties to agree in the contract on:

  • The advance level and the number of advances;
  • The time of the advance;
  • Whether or not there is an advance guarantee;
  • The amount recovered from each payment.

Because the law leaves it to the parties, homeowners should state these items with specific figures. The mandatory levels in section 3 below apply to public investment and PPP projects, but can serve as a reference when negotiating.

3. Advance levels for public investment and PPP projects

Under Clause 3, Article 12:

  • An advance is made only after the contract takes effect and the employer has received the advance guarantee (if required); a construction contract must also have a site clearance plan or a site handover record.
  • The advance may not exceed 30% of the contract price at signing (including contingency, if any); a higher level must be permitted by the person deciding on the investment.
  • Minimum level for construction works contracts: 10% if the contract price is above VND 50 billion; 15% if from VND 10 billion to 50 billion; 20% if under VND 10 billion.
  • Minimum level for consulting contracts: 15% if above VND 10 billion; 20% up to VND 10 billion. Supply of materials and equipment, EPC, turnkey and other contracts: 10%.
  • For the part of the advance above the minimum level, the corresponding part of the contract price is not subject to price adjustment from the time of the advance.

4. When is an advance guarantee required?

For public investment and PPP projects, where the advance value is greater than VND 1 billion, the contractor must submit an advance guarantee of equivalent value and currency before receiving the money. A guarantee is not required if the advance is VND 1 billion or less or the contract is in the self-performance form (Point c, Clause 3, Article 12).

The advance guarantee remains valid until the advance is fully recovered and decreases gradually by the amount recovered (Point d, Clause 3, Article 12). For a private house, the homeowner may agree to require a bank guarantee when the advance is large, to reduce the risk of a contractor taking the money and abandoning the work.

5. Recovery of the advance and use for the proper purpose

  • The advance is recovered gradually through payments; the amount recovered each time is set by the parties in the contract, but it must be fully recovered when the cumulative payment value (including the advance and completed work) reaches 80% of the contract price (Point m, Clause 3, Article 12).
  • The contractor must use the advance for the proper purpose and proper recipients (Point đ, Clause 3, Article 12).
  • High-value components, semi-finished products or materials that need to be stocked may be agreed for a separate advance, outside the contract advance (Point e, Clause 3, Article 12).

6. Suggestions for homeowners drafting the advance clause

  1. State the advance amount or percentage and the time of the transfer.
  2. State how the advance is deducted at each payment (for example, deducted at a percentage in each installment).
  3. If the advance is large, require an advance guarantee or split it into several payments by progress.
  4. All proposals and requests between the two parties should be in writing (Clause 4, Article 4).

Before signing the construction contract, the homeowner should check that the conditions to start construction are met - see Conditions to start building a house 2026.

This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. The advance levels and advance guarantee in sections 3 and 4 are mandatory for contracts of public investment and PPP projects. For contracts signed and being performed before 1 July 2026 (projects not using public investment capital), the parties may agree to apply Decree 210/2026/ND-CP; if they cannot agree, the rules at the time of signing apply (Clause 2, Article 33).
