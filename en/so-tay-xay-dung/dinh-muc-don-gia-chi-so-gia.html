Construction Norms, Unit Prices and Price Indices 2026: How to Apply

The three “price measuring” tools of the construction industry – norms, prices and price indices – after Circulars 37/2026 and 38/2026 replaced the 2021 set of norms.

Whether an estimate is right or wrong depends largely on three tools: norms (the inputs needed for one unit of work), prices (of materials, labour, machine shifts and composite construction prices) and price indices (price movements over time). From 1 July 2026, this system is set out in Decree 206/2026/ND-CP, Circular 37/2026/TT-BXD and Circular 38/2026/TT-BXD.

What does the construction norm system include?

Type of normContentUsed for
Basic normsNorms for material use, labour productivity, and productivity of construction machinery and equipmentDetermining or adjusting estimate norms
Estimate normsInputs of materials, labour and machines to complete one unit of quantity of work, consistent with technical requirements, conditions and construction methodsDetermining construction prices, preparing and managing costs
Cost normsNorms expressed as a percentage or as a valueCalculating indirect costs, pre-calculated taxable income, project management, consulting and some other costs
Work-specific estimate normsNorms specific to a workLarge-scale, technically complex projects, new technology or materials not yet in the norm system

Source: Article 19 of Decree 206/2026. Economic-technical norms comprise basic norms and estimate norms.

The current set of norms under Circular 38/2026/TT-BXD

Circular 38/2026/TT-BXD takes effect on 1 July 2026 and replaces Circular 12/2021/TT-BXD, Circular 09/2024/TT-BXD, Circular 08/2025/TT-BXD and Article 2 of Circular 60/2025/TT-BXD (Article 2). The norms issued with it (Article 1):

  1. Estimate norms for construction surveys (Appendix I).
  2. Estimate norms for construction works (Appendix II).
  3. Estimate norms for installing technical systems of works (Appendix III).
  4. Estimate norms for installing machinery and technological equipment (Appendix IV).
  5. Estimate norms for specialized construction testing (Appendix V).
  6. Estimate norms for repair and maintenance of construction works (Appendix VI).
  7. Norms for use of construction materials (Appendix VII).
  8. Norms for project management and construction consulting costs (Appendix VIII).

The detailed content of the norm appendices consists of very large tables of figures and is not yet in our library – check the original from the Ministry of Construction when preparing estimates.

Who issues norms; applying, adjusting and developing new norms

  • The Ministry of Construction issues norms for nationwide use; ministries managing specialized works issue norms for specialized work not in the Ministry of Construction's system; provincial People's Committees issue norms for locally specific work not issued by the two higher levels (Clause 1, Article 20).
  • When preparing total investment and estimates, the preparer may apply, adapt and adjust norms on the basis of the issued norm system, or determine norms based on the intended technology, methods and construction conditions (Point a, Clause 5, Article 20).
  • During construction, the investor of a public investment project surveys and determines work-specific norms for work that has no norm or whose norm is unsuitable, and sends them to the Ministry of Construction and the issuing agency for review (Points b and c, Clause 5, Article 20). The method for determining new norms and adjusting norms follows Appendix I of Circular 37/2026/TT-BXD (Clause 2, Article 10 of Circular 36/2026).
  • Large-scale public investment projects and those with new technology may use foreign norms after translation, analysis and evaluation and the investor's decision; during construction, verification data must be collected (Point d, Clause 5, Article 20).
  • Norms issued by specialized ministries and provincial People's Committees before 1 July 2026 must have the labour input component converted to the labour groups prescribed in Circular 37/2026 before 1 September 2026 (Clause 1, Article 10 of Circular 36/2026).

Construction prices and prices of cost elements

Construction prices comprise work prices, work group prices, prices of structural parts and units, cost rates and investment capital rates. Work prices are determined from estimate norms and the prices of direct cost elements (materials, labour, machine shifts), or from quotations and the prices of similar works (Article 22 of Decree 206/2026). The Department of Construction (as decentralized or authorized by the provincial People's Committee) publishes the prices of cost elements on the following schedule (Clause 3, Article 24, Article 11 of Circular 36/2026):

Type of pricePublication cyclePublication time
Construction material pricesMonthly or quarterly; materials with unusual fluctuations published monthlyQuarterly: before the 15th of the first month of the following quarter; monthly: before the 10th of the following month
Construction labour pricesAnnually, or earlier on unusual fluctuationsBefore 15 January of the following year
Construction machine and equipment shift pricesAnnually, or earlier when neededBefore 15 January of the following year
  • Published material prices are the average price during the month by brand and manufacturer; if there is no fluctuation in the next period, the guidance of the previous period is used for reference (Clause 3, Article 11).
  • Published labour prices include wages, allowances by the characteristics of construction production and certain amounts payable by workers (social, health and unemployment insurance, trade union fees, etc.) (Clause 4, Article 11).
  • Where a published price is unavailable or unsuitable for the work, the investor or consultant determines the price under Appendix IV of Circular 36/2026 and Circular 37/2026; the results of determining labour and machine shift prices are sent to the Department of Construction for monitoring (Article 12 of Circular 36/2026).

Labour prices for 2026 – 2027

Under Article 2 of Circular 37/2026/TT-BXD: where a locality has not yet determined labour prices under the new method, the Department of Construction converts the previously published price table using the method in Appendix III for publication and application from 1 July 2026; at the same time it surveys and determines labour prices under the new method for publication and application from 1 January 2027. For old norms whose labour groups have not been converted, select the suitable labour group under Table 3.1 of Appendix III. Estimators in early 2027 should check whether the Department of Construction where the work is located has published the new labour price table.

Construction price indices and contract price adjustment

  • The construction price index reflects price movements over time and is used to determine and adjust total investment, estimates, package prices and the contract price. It includes indices by type of work, by cost structure (construction, equipment, other costs), by cost element (materials, labour, machines) and price indices for certain principal materials (Clauses 1 and 2, Article 23).
  • The Ministry of Construction publishes the national price index annually; the Department of Construction determines the local price index for publication by the provincial People's Committee (or the Department as authorized). Publication times: quarterly before the 15th of the first month of the following quarter; annually before 15 January of the following year; monthly (if any) before the 10th of the following month. The unit is % (Clause 3, Article 23, Article 13 of Circular 36/2026).
  • The investor decides whether to use the price index published by the Ministry of Construction or the provincial People's Committee (if suitable) or to determine its own (it may hire a consultant) under the Ministry of Construction's method to adjust the contract price (Clause 4, Article 23).
  • For contracts of public investment and PPP projects: adjustment by direct offsetting or a price adjustment coefficient (Pn); the source of the price index and the method must be specifically agreed in the contract; payment of the agreed adjustment value does not require signing a contract amendment (Points dd, e and g, Clause 2, Article 24 of Decree 210/2026).
  • The method for determining the construction price index is in Appendix V of Circular 37/2026/TT-BXD (Article 1).

Investment capital rates, cost rates, structural part prices

  • Investment capital rate: the cost needed for one unit of area, volume, length, capacity or service capability of a project or work; cost rate: the cost per unit of measure used to determine the cost of a technical system, item, construction cost or equipment cost (Points d and dd, Clause 1, Article 22).
  • The Ministry of Construction periodically publishes capital rates, cost rates, structural part prices and the national price index; specialized ministries and provincial People's Committees publish those that are missing or unsuitable; publication is periodic, annually (Clauses 1, 2 and 3, Article 24, Clause 1, Article 11 of Circular 36).
  • Capital rates are commonly used to prepare the preliminary total investment (VSB = P × SSVĐT × kĐC + GBT,TĐC + C + GDP – formula 1.1 of Appendix I of Circular 36/2026, see consolidated text). The Ministry of Construction has Decision 425/QD-BXD publishing the 2025 construction investment capital rates and composite construction prices (full text not yet in the library).

Norms for maintenance and repair of works

Maintenance costs (upkeep, repair and other work in the maintenance plan) are determined under Circular 40/2026/TT-BXD: upkeep costs are estimated by quantities and upkeep work prices (Article 3); the preparer may apply, adapt and adjust maintenance norms issued by the competent authority and, where none exist, determine them by the Ministry of Construction's method (Article 6). Estimate norms for repair and upkeep of works are in Appendix VI of Circular 38/2026.

Notes on transition and for private projects

  • Norms, construction prices, labour prices and machine shift prices issued or published before 1 July 2026 continue to apply, for reference, until newly reviewed and issued ones take effect (Clause 7, Article 36 of Decree 206/2026).
  • Private projects are not required to use state norms, but published norms and prices are an objective “yardstick” for checking quotations, negotiating unit prices and resolving disputes.
  • When agreeing price adjustment in a private contract, it is advisable to state clearly: the source of the price index (which provincial Department of Construction, which index), the base period, the adjustment period and the formula.

This summary is for reference only and does not replace legal documents. Decree 206/2026/ND-CP is mandatory for public investment, PPP and other budget capital projects; other projects use it for reference. Circular 36/2026/TT-BXD is “partially expired” and has been corrected by Decision 1538/QD-BXD (use the consolidated text 89/2026/VBHN-TT-BXD). Material, labour and machine shift prices and price indices must be taken from the publication of the Department of Construction where the work is located at the time of preparing the estimate.
