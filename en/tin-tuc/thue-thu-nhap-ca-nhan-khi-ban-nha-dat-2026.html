Personal Income Tax on Selling Property in Vietnam 2026: 2%

Quan Construction · 11/11/2026

From 1 July 2026, personal income tax (PIT) on selling houses and land follows Personal Income Tax Law No. 109/2025/QH15 (replacing Law 04/2007/QH12) and Decree 253/2026/ND-CP (replacing Decree 65/2013/ND-CP). Below are the points homeowners need to know before signing a contract.

1. What sales are subject to PIT?

Under Clause 5, Article 3 of the PIT Law and Article 11 of Decree 253/2026/ND-CP, income from the transfer of real estate includes:

  • Transfer of land use rights, or of land together with attached assets (houses, including housing to be formed in the future; construction works; crops and livestock attached to the land).
  • Transfer of ownership or use rights of houses, including housing to be formed in the future.
  • Transfer of land lease rights and water surface lease rights.
  • Other income from real estate transfer, including capital contribution in the form of land and houses to an enterprise; and cases of authorization where the authorized person has full rights like an owner.

2. Calculation: 2% of the transfer price

Under Article 14 of the PIT Law, tax = transfer price × 2%. This rate applies to both resident and non-resident individuals (Article 24). Tax is calculated on the selling price, without deducting the purchase price or costs, so even a sale at a loss is taxed unless exempt.

Example: a house sold at a contract price of VND 3 billion has PIT of 3,000,000,000 × 2% = VND 60 million.

3. If a low price is stated in the contract, which price is used?

The transfer price is the price stated in the contract at the time of transfer. However, under Clauses 2 and 3, Article 57 of Decree 253/2026/ND-CP:

  • If the contract does not state a land price or states one lower than the land price table (multiplied by the adjustment coefficient, if any), the taxable land price is the price under the land price table.
  • If the value of the house or works stated is lower than the price used to calculate the house registration fee issued by the provincial People's Committee, the registration fee price is used.

Stating a price lower than the real price does not reduce tax below the level under the price table, and it creates risk in a dispute. The buyer also pays a registration fee - see Land and house registration fee 2026.

4. Tax-exempt cases

a) Transfer, gift or inheritance between relatives (Clause 1, Article 4 of the PIT Law; Article 18 of Decree 253/2026/ND-CP): between husband and wife; biological parents and biological children; adoptive parents and adopted children; parents-in-law and daughter-in-law or son-in-law (even if the spouse has died); paternal or maternal grandparents and grandchildren; and siblings. Real estate divided on divorce under an agreement or a court judgment is also exempt.

b) A sole home or residential land plot (Clause 2, Article 4; Article 19 of Decree 253/2026/ND-CP). The seller must meet all of the following:

  1. At the time of sale, the seller has only one house or one residential land plot in Vietnam. If the seller still has a house or work to be formed in the future, it does not count as the sole one.
  2. Has held ownership or use rights for at least 183 days up to the time of sale, counted from the date the certificate was issued (for reissued or exchanged certificates, from the date of the earlier certificate).
  3. Sells the entire house and land; if only a part is sold, that part is not exempt.

For co-owned houses (including spouses): only a person who has no other house or residential land elsewhere is exempt. The sole-home exemption does not apply when selling a house or work to be formed in the future. The seller declares and is responsible for the declaration; a false declaration leads to back-collection of tax and penalties.

5. Who pays the tax and when is it calculated?

  • The taxpayer is the seller. The parties may agree in the contract that the buyer pays on the seller's behalf; the tax calculation time is then when procedures to register land use rights or ownership are carried out (Clause 5, Article 57).
  • With no agreement for payment on behalf: the tax calculation time is when the transfer contract takes effect (Clause 2, Article 14 of the Law).
  • For co-owned houses: tax is divided by ownership share; if there are no documents determining shares, it is divided equally; one person may be authorized to pay as representative (Point a, Clause 6, Article 57).
  • Tax is calculated for each transfer that occurs (Article 66). Exchanging houses or land between individuals must also be declared and taxed.

6. Gifting property to non-relatives: 10% tax

Receiving real estate by inheritance or gift from a person who is not among the relatives in section 4 is taxed to the recipient at 10% on the part of the value exceeding VND 20 million per receipt (Article 18 of the PIT Law).

7. Steps homeowners should take

  1. Check whether you fall under an exemption (relatives, sole home) and prepare documents to prove it.
  2. State the true price in the contract; consult the provincial land price table and registration fee price to know the minimum.
  3. Agree clearly in the contract who pays PIT and the registration fee - see also Notarizing a property sale contract.
  4. Declare tax with the tax authority under tax administration law when carrying out the title transfer.
This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. Documents, filing deadlines and tax exemption follow tax administration law (not in our legal library). PIT Law 109/2025/QH15 is shown as partly no longer in force on the National Legal Portal; the articles cited above are guided by Decree 253/2026/ND-CP (still in force), but when transacting, ask the tax authority where the property is located.
