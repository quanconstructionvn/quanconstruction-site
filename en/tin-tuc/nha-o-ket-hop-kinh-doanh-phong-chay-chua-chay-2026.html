Mixed-Use Homes 2026: Fire Safety Conditions for Home Businesses

Quan Construction · 30/10/2026

Many families live and also run a shop, small workshop or storeroom in the same house. Law No. 55/2024/QH15 on Fire Prevention, Fighting and Rescue (the Fire Law, effective from 1 July 2025) has a separate article for this type of home: Article 21 - Fire prevention for homes combined with production and business. This article summarizes what homeowners and tenants of business premises need to do, with links to the original text.

1. Who is responsible for ensuring fire safety?

  • The household head directly using the home must comply with Article 20 (homes) and Article 21 (homes combined with production and business), and regularly self-inspect and remedy fire and explosion risks (Clause 6, Article 8).
  • A tenant, borrower or occupant of a home combined with production and business is also responsible for ensuring the fire safety conditions under Article 21; the landlord is responsible for promoting, urging and reminding the tenant (Clause 8, Article 8).
  • If the home is on the list of establishments subject to fire safety management, it is handled as an establishment under Article 23 (Clause 4, Article 20); the household head is then the head of the establishment (Clause 11, Article 2).

When renting out premises, state clearly in the contract which party provides the fire-fighting equipment and carries out the monthly self-check.

2. General conditions for every home combined with business

Under Clause 1, Article 21, a home combined with production or business must ensure:

  1. All the fire prevention conditions for homes in Article 20: safe electrical equipment, a safely arranged cooking area and worship area, suitable fire-fighting equipment, and maintained escape routes and emergency exits. For details see Fire safety requirements for homes 2026.
  2. Prohibition signs, warning signs and direction signs as required.
  3. Areas producing or trading goods with fire or explosion risk must have separation or fire-barrier solutions from the living area.

If electricity is used for production (machinery, a small workshop), electrical safety rules for production must also be followed; electrical equipment used in environments with fire or explosion hazards must be specialized equipment (Clause 2, Article 24).

3. Trading goods hazardous for fire and explosion: higher requirements

Goods hazardous for fire and explosion are goods on the list prescribed by the Government (Clause 9, Article 2). A home combined with production or trade of such goods must, in addition to the conditions in section 2 (Clause 2, Article 21):

  • Have no sleeping area in the production or business area;
  • Have fire alarm equipment, ventilation solutions and leak detection devices for gases hazardous for fire and explosion, appropriate to the function and characteristics of the house;
  • Keep the production or business area fire-separated from the escape route of the living area.

If you are unsure whether an item is on the list, ask the commune or ward police before arranging the premises.

4. Renovating a house for business: design notes

  • The Law strictly prohibits converting or adding functions to works or work items that do not ensure fire safety (Clause 8, Article 14).
  • When converting part or all of a private house to another function together with repair or renovation, the design and construction must comply with regulations and standards and suit the new function, and comply with fire safety law (Clauses 5 and 6, Article 10 of Decree 207/2026/ND-CP).
  • You must not obstruct escape routes or disable escape routes or fire spread barriers; and must not move or hide installed fire-fighting equipment, signs or direction signs without authorization (Clause 10, Article 14).

5. Self-inspection and government inspection

  • Homes combined with production or business are subject to fire safety inspection (Clause 1, Article 11).
  • The household head or tenant, borrower or occupant self-inspects regularly; they decide the timing, scope and way of recording results but no less often than once a month (Clause 1, Article 14 of Decree 105/2025/ND-CP).
  • The commune-level People's Committee manages and inspects fire safety for homes combined with production or business (Point a, Clause 2, Article 42) and conducts surprise inspections when there are signs of violations, complaints or denunciations, or as required to ensure security and order (Point c, Clause 2, Article 13).

6. Quick monthly self-check list

  1. Wires, sockets and machinery are safe; fire extinguishers are still usable and kept where they are easy to reach.
  2. Escape routes and stairs are not blocked by goods; signs and direction signs are still complete.
  3. The business area is separated or fire-separated from the living area.
  4. For goods hazardous for fire and explosion: nobody sleeps in the business area; alarms, ventilation and leak detection devices work well.
This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. The 2024 Fire Law and Decree 105/2025/ND-CP have had some articles amended (including by Decree 347/2026/ND-CP); specific conditions also depend on technical regulations and the type of goods traded. When applying them, check the current documents and ask the police or People's Committee of the commune or ward where the house is located.
