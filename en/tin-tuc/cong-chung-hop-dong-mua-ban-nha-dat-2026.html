Notarizing a Property Sale Contract in Vietnam 2026

Quan Construction · 04/11/2026

When buying or selling land and a house, a contract that has not been notarized or authenticated is not yet effective and the certificate cannot be transferred. This article summarizes the rules under Notarization Law No. 46/2024/QH15 (effective from 1 July 2025), the 2023 Housing Law and the 2024 Land Law.

1. Which contracts must be notarized or authenticated?

  • Housing: contracts for sale, lease-purchase, gift, exchange, capital contribution or mortgage of a house must be notarized or authenticated (Clause 1, Article 164 of the Housing Law).
  • Land and houses attached to land: contracts for transfer, gift, mortgage or capital contribution of land use rights, or of land use rights and assets attached to land, must be notarized or authenticated (Point a, Clause 3, Article 27 of the Land Law).
  • Not mandatory (notarized only if the parties wish): lease, loan or free accommodation of a house; sale of a house where one party is an organization (social housing, resettlement housing, etc.); a transfer where one party is a real estate business organization (Clause 2, Article 164; Point b, Clause 3, Article 27).

For mandatory transactions, the contract takes effect at the time notarization or authentication is completed (Clause 1, Article 164). A notarized document has evidentiary value, and the facts in it need not be proved, unless it is declared invalid by a Court (Article 6 of the Notarization Law). Before notarization, check that the property is eligible for the transaction - see Conditions for buying, selling or gifting a house 2026.

2. Notarization or authentication, and where?

  • Notarization at a Notary Office or Notary Practice Office; authentication at the commune-level People's Committee where the house is located (Clause 4, Article 164 of the Housing Law).
  • A notary may notarize real estate transactions only within the province or city where the notarial practice organization has its head office: a sale contract for property in a given province is notarized in that province; powers of attorney on real estate, wills, etc. are not subject to this limit (Article 44).
  • Notarization is done at the head office of the notarial practice organization; it may be done outside the office only in certain cases, such as when a person cannot travel for health reasons or is receiving inpatient treatment (Article 46).

3. Documents to prepare

Under Clause 1, Article 42 of the Notarization Law, the person requesting notarization submits 1 set of documents (online, in person or by post) consisting of:

  1. The draft contract; if there is none, the notary can be asked to draft it (Article 43).
  2. A copy of identity documents: citizen identity card, passport or other documents identifying the person.
  3. A copy of the certificate of land use rights and house ownership (sổ đỏ, sổ hồng) or substitute documents as prescribed.
  4. A copy of other relevant documents required by law.

Information that the notary can retrieve from the National Population Database need not be submitted as documents (but a data-retrieval fee is paid). The originals must be brought at signing for comparison (Clauses 1 and 7, Article 42). For land held jointly by several people that cannot be divided by shares, the members sign together or authorize a representative (Clause 2, Article 27 of the Land Law).

4. Steps of notarizing the contract

  1. The notary examines the documents; a refusal must state the reasons.
  2. The notary explains the rights, obligations and legal consequences; if there are signs of threat or coercion or unclear points that cannot be clarified, the notary refuses to notarize.
  3. The draft is reviewed; clauses contrary to law must be amended, otherwise notarization is refused.
  4. The parties re-read the contract and, if they agree, sign every page in front of the notary; the signing is photographed for the file (Clause 1, Article 50).
  5. The notary compares the originals and signs the certification; the notary office applies its seal and hands the originals to the parties (Clauses 2-8, Article 42).

5. How long does it take and what are the fees?

Notarization takes no more than 2 working days from receipt of a valid application; complex transactions no more than 10 working days, not counting time for verification or expert appraisal (Article 45). The person requesting notarization pays the notarization fee under law on fees and charges (Article 70).

After notarization, the parties carry out title transfer and pay taxes and fees; see also Land and house registration fee 2026.

6. Amending or cancelling a notarized contract

Amending, supplementing or terminating a notarized contract requires a written agreement of all the persons who signed, and is done at the notarial practice organization where it was notarized, unless the law provides otherwise (Article 53).

7. What is new from 1 January 2027

Law No. 04/2026/QH16 amending the Notarization Law is not yet in force (effective from 1 January 2027). When it takes effect, documents in the file may be electronic copies or electronic originals; the Government will set a roadmap for notarizing real estate transactions nationwide. For now, the rules above still apply.

This article is only a summary for reference and does not replace the legal documents. The 2024 Notarization Law has had some articles amended by Law No. 106/2025/QH15 (not in our legal library); the 2023 Housing Law and 2024 Land Law have also had some articles amended. When transacting, check the current documents and ask the notarial practice organization and the People's Committee of the commune or ward where the property is located directly.
